Your two questions

Same recipe to first crack, then diverge

Are we keeping FC the same and only extending development?

Yes. Every move through first crack is identical, including the heater at 60% from 7:30 onward. Do not add moves after FC. The only thing that changes is the stopwatch from FC to DROP. Drop temperature is an outcome, and the projected drops below exist only to confirm the long batch stays clear of the 408F eject. If FC comes late on a batch, that is fine: still count from the moment you mark it.

Can an extended warm-up compensate for later batches running hotter?

Partly, and the direction matters. Your logs show later batches on the same day reach FC about 35 seconds sooner, and the environment probe does not predict it: the drum body is what carries the heat, not the air. So make batch 1 look like batch 2 rather than the reverse. Overshoot the empty drum, cool it to the charge point with the heater off, then charge, and use that same cool-then-charge routine between batches so all three start from the same state. Whatever drift is left is absorbed by timing from FC instead of from charge. Artisan's between-batch protocol and follow-background PID can take this over later.

Microphone

Run the ear with alerts on for all three

The ear records every batch, logs the clicks it hears, and when it counts seven cracks inside 20 seconds it prints a bold FIRST CRACK line in its own terminal and rings the bell. The alert comes from the ear, not from Artisan, and it marks nothing in Artisan. You still press FCs. The alert fires about 15 to 20 seconds after the burst actually starts, so it is a confirmation, not the earliest signal; the analyzer records the true onset afterwards regardless of when you pressed.

  1. Already set up on the roaster: ~/.local/bin/run_ear and its settings in ~/CodeProjects/ear/ear.conf (device, paths, rsync target). Nothing to configure on the day. Do not start the GoPro sentinel; both want port 8765.
  2. Clip the capsule to the plastic front housing beside the bean window, foam windscreen on, pointed away from the fan exhaust. Route the USB cable away from the Hottop's mains lead.
  3. Before pressing ON in Artisan, in a terminal on the roaster: 
    cd ~/CodeProjects/ear && run_ear ear.py listen --bean "Rwanda Rusizi Gaseke"
    Then press ON. The status box should say Artisan: connected and Rec: with a file name.
  4. With the drum running and no beans, set the capture gain in alsamixer -c 1 so the Peak line reads about -30 dBFS. If the box ever shows CLIPPING, turn the gain down before the next batch.
  5. Keep the ear's terminal where you can see and hear it. Leave it running through OFF; the sidecar and WAV push to the dev machine on their own. Start a fresh listen for each batch, after Artisan's OFF and before the next ON.
  6. If capture fails, the box says Rec: FAILED. Roast anyway; the batch still counts. Run run_ear ear.py devices afterward to see what PortAudio found.
Before the first charge

Soak the drum, then keep the gaps fixed

  1. Press ON in Artisan at the start of preheat, not at charge, so the warm-up is logged.
  2. Preheat with the drum turning and the heater at 100%, and overshoot to about 370F BT instead of stopping at 300F. The drum body soaks while the empty drum climbs.
  3. Heater 0%, fan 100%, drum on. Let BT fall to 300F. Heater back to 100% and charge batch 1. Write down the ET reading at that moment.
  4. After each drop, the same routine: heater 0%, fan 100%, drum on, let BT fall to 300F, heater to 100%. Charge when BT is at 300F and ET is within about 10F of the batch 1 reading. Waiting for both is the compensation.
  5. Name every roast in Artisan before you press ON. Batch #15 went into history as "Roaster Scope" and cannot be grouped with its bean.

Marking FC on a quiet bean

This Rwanda cracks softly. Mark FC at the first distinct crack, or at 363F, whichever comes first. Do not wait for a rolling chorus. Two additions from the first session: if the ear's alert fires before you have marked, mark immediately; and do not mark below about 355F unless the alert has already fired. Every real first crack on this bean has landed between 358F and 366F, and the one early mark so far was at 349F. Set two Artisan alarms: 356F to start listening, 363F as the fallback mark. The same rule on all three batches keeps the contrast real.

FC has landed anywhere from 9:16 to 10:36 on this recipe. That spread is the machine, not you. Time from the mark, never from charge. A drop of 3 to 7 F/min in the RoR within 20 seconds of your mark means you marked well.

At the table

Taste for difference, not for words

  1. Rest 3 to 5 days before judging. At 24 hours all three will taste flat and gassy in the Switch.
  2. Brew all three in the Hario Switch the same way: same dose, same grind, same water, same steep, back to back. Label the cups by batch number only.
  3. Triangle first. Pour two cups of one batch and one of another, shuffle, and pick the odd one out. If you can, you have discrimination and only lack vocabulary. If you cannot, the bracket is too narrow, not your palate: widen it next time.
  4. Then taste 1, 2, 3 in order and answer the intake below for each. Preference is the signal. Naming why is optional.

Sour and thin point to under-development; roasty and ashy to over. Astringency is the one you already catch reliably. On this bean the vendor promises brown sugar, cocoa, spice, and sweet tea. Sweetness and a clean finish are the marks of landing it, and neither needs a wheel.
