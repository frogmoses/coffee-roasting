Roast day sheet

Burundi Monge Murambi Hill profile v1

This Burundi is a dense, high-grown washed Bourbon, close kin to the Rwanda Rusizi Gaseke: same process, same variety, same bean density, and the same charge and warm-up. So version 1 keeps the Rwanda schedule exactly: the same fan steps and the same four heater cuts at the same bean temperatures. On the Rwanda, development of about 2:15 to 2:30 after first crack tasted balanced and best; under about 1:40 it was sour, and past about 3:10 roasty. This plan's 2:32 sits in that window and suits a City+ roast that keeps the tea-like brightness and honey sweetness. First crack can land up to a minute away from the prediction on a bean roasted here for the first time, so nothing else changes yet; the three batches test development instead, and this first session finds the Burundi's own best development time. Expect in the cup: A balanced cup, neither sour nor roasty, sweet from start to finish with cane sugar and honey, brisk tea-like acidity with orange and citrus-rind brightness, syrupy body, and only mild astringency: the City+ roast where this Burundi shows its brightness without going flat.

Before the first charge

Load the set, start the ear, let the warm-up run

  1. Before Artisan opens on the roaster, its settings must match the copy kept on your dev machine (that copy has the Control On button and Artisan's quantifiers off): DEPLOY_SSH_HOST=roaster artisan/deploy.sh diff prints nothing; if it does, push with Artisan closed, then start Artisan.
  2. Check the microphone: run_ear ear.py level with the empty drum running. Peaks near -30 dBFS and no clipping; snap your fingers by the capsule and the cracks column climbs. Adjust the capture gain in alsamixer -c 1 until it does.
  3. Start the ear on the roaster: run_ear ear.py listen --bean "Burundi Monge Murambi Hill". One listen covers the day.
  4. Name the roast in Artisan (Burundi Monge Murambi Hill) before you press ON, so the batch groups with its bean.
  5. Config → Alarms → Load profile-v1-bt.alrm. Check it against the alarm table at the end of this sheet.
  6. ON. The first alarm row takes Hottop control (super-user comes with it) and starts the drum; the next closes the door, stops the stirrer, and sets heater 100% and fan 0%. Do not press CONTROL yourself, today or ever: releasing control turns the Hottop off.
  7. The alarms run the warm-up: heater 100% until BT reaches 370F, then heater 0% and fan 100% until BT falls to 300F, then fan 0% and heater 100% with a pop-up that says charge. Charge as soon as it does; write the ET reading on the batch's intake line (it is a record, not a thing to wait for).
  8. Press START right before you charge, then CHARGE. From here the alarms make every move through first crack. If the timer is not running you have not pressed START and no alarm is watching: DROP and start over.
  9. DROP is the panic button. Never OFF during a roast: with recording off no alarm evaluates, the 398F drop included, and the heater stays where it was.
  10. Between batches, in this order: DROP; COOL END (always, before OFF: it closes the door and stops the drum, fan, and stirrer; after OFF nothing will); OFF; save the log (the ear waits for that save before it can start the next batch's session); then ON. The warm-up repeats on its own.

First crack

Mark it, then the timer runs

  1. You mark FCs, at the first distinct crack or at 363F BT, whichever comes first. The ear's bell is confirmation, about 18 s behind the first cracks; if it rings before you have marked, mark now. The 372F pop-up means you have missed it: mark at once.
  2. Batch 1: start a stopwatch at your FCs mark and press DROP at 2:32 (that is 152 seconds, read the stopwatch as minutes:seconds).
  3. Batch 2: start a stopwatch at your FCs mark and press DROP at 2:00 (that is 120 seconds, read the stopwatch as minutes:seconds).
  4. Batch 3: start a stopwatch at your FCs mark and press DROP at 3:00 (that is 180 seconds, read the stopwatch as minutes:seconds).
  5. Drop early if BT reaches 400F; the 398F alarm will do it for you.
  6. Watch for: First crack may come up to about a minute either side of 10:33, because the times come from the Rwanda. The heater cuts follow bean temperature, so they still land right; time the drop from your FCs mark, not the clock.
  7. Watch for: The beans should bottom out near 160-170F about a minute after charge, and be climbing again before the fan step to 20% at 1:30.
  8. Watch for: Astringency: on the Rwanda it went away when the first heater cut came earlier. If this cup tastes drying, move the 262F cut earlier in the next version.
  9. Watch for: The 2:32 batch should drop near 381F, well under the 398F automatic drop and the 408F eject.
At the table

Taste for difference, not for words

  1. Rest 3 to 5 days before judging. At 24 hours every batch tastes flat and gassy in the Switch.
  2. Brew all batches in the Hario Switch the same way, back to back, cups labeled by batch number only. Recipe: 15 g medium grind, 250 g at 94°C, switch closed; pour 50 g and swirl at 0:00, fill to 250 g at 0:45, open the switch at 2:00, drained by about 3:00. Hot water throughout: no cool second pour, since that hides the roasty end of the scale.
  3. Taste each cup hot at about four minutes and again ten minutes later. Sourness, astringency, and whether sweetness holds all show up in the cooler cup.
  4. Triangle first. Pour two cups of one batch and one of another, shuffle, and pick the odd one out. If you can, you have discrimination and only lack vocabulary. If you cannot, the batches were too close together, not your palate at fault.
  5. Then taste in roasting order and answer the intake below for each. Preference is the signal. Naming why is optional.
