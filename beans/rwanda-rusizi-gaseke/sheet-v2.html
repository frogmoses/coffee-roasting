The staircase is proven and I keep it untouched — it delivers balanced, low-astringency roasts at a controlled rate into FC. Before the first charge

Load the set, start the ear, let the warm-up run

  1. Name the roast in Artisan (Rwanda Rusizi Gaseke) before you press ON, so the batch groups with its bean.
  2. Config → Alarms → Load profile-v2.alrm. Check the table matches the list below.
  3. Start the ear on the roaster: run_ear ear.py listen --bean "Rwanda Rusizi Gaseke". One listen covers the day.
  4. ON, then right-click the timer LCD until the message bar says super on, then CONTROL once (Hottop control turned on). Never press CONTROL again today: releasing control turns the Hottop off.
  5. The alarms run the warm-up: drum motor on, heater 100% until BT reaches 370F, then heater 0% and fan 100% until BT falls to 300F, then fan 0% and heater 100% with a pop-up that says charge. Note the ET reading at that moment.
  6. Press START right before you charge, then CHARGE. From here the alarms make every move through first crack.
  7. Between batches: DROP, COOL END (the drum stops), OFF, save the profile, then ON again. The warm-up repeats on its own. Charge when BT is at the charge point and ET is within about 10F of the batch-1 reading.

First crack

Mark it, then the timer runs

  1. The ear rings its bell about 15-20 s after the first cracks; it is a confirmation, not the earliest signal. Mark FCs at the first distinct crack or at 363F BT, whichever comes first.
  2. Batch 1: start a stopwatch at FCs and press DROP at 2:30 (that is 150 seconds, read the stopwatch as minutes:seconds).
  3. Batch 2: start a stopwatch at FCs and press DROP at 2:10 (that is 130 seconds, read the stopwatch as minutes:seconds).
  4. Batch 3: start a stopwatch at FCs and press DROP at 2:50 (that is 170 seconds, read the stopwatch as minutes:seconds).
  5. Drop early if BT reaches 400F; the 398F alarm will do it for you.
  6. Watch for: Heat-soak/ambient: later batches or a warmer day pull FC 15-35 s earlier; trust the BT-triggered cuts, not the clock.
  7. Watch for: Drop BT should land near 380F — if FC BT runs high, the 150 s dev could push past Full City; shorten dev rather than let it darken.
  8. Watch for: Astringency: if it returns, the h80 cut is the lever to move earlier next revision.
  9. Watch for: FC declaration lag ~18 s from the ear detector; start the 150 s dev clock from the machine's FCs mark, not the first audible crack.
At the table

Taste for difference, not for words

  1. Rest 3 to 5 days before judging. At 24 hours every batch tastes flat and gassy in the Switch.
  2. Brew all batches in the Hario Switch the same way, back to back, cups labeled by batch number only. Recipe: 15 g medium grind, 250 g at 94°C, switch closed; pour 50 g and swirl at 0:00, fill to 250 g at 0:45, open the switch at 2:00, drained by about 3:00. Hot water throughout: no cool second pour, since that hides the roasty end of the scale.
  3. Taste each cup hot at about four minutes and again ten minutes later. Sourness, astringency, and whether sweetness holds all show up in the cooler cup.
  4. Triangle first. Pour two cups of one batch and one of another, shuffle, and pick the odd one out. If you can, you have discrimination and only lack vocabulary. If you cannot, the bracket is too narrow, not your palate.
  5. Then taste in roasting order and answer the intake below for each. Preference is the signal. Naming why is optional.
